# English Sounds Guide
*For Spanish speakers — B1/B2 level*
 
---
 
## How to use this guide
 
Each section explains one difficult sound in English. You will find:
- A short explanation of how to make the sound
- The key difference from Spanish
- A table with practice words
**IPA symbols used:** `/r/` `/l/` `/ɪ/` `/iː/` `/aɪ/` `/ʃ/` `/dʒ/`
 
---
 
## 1. The R sound — /r/
 
**How to make it:** Round your lips a little. Raise the middle of your tongue toward the roof of your mouth, but do not touch it. Push air softly from your throat.
 
**Key difference:** The English R never vibrates. The Spanish R vibrates (like in *roca* or *perro*). The English R is soft and smooth.

## 2. The L sound — /l/
 
English has **two** L sounds. Spanish only has one.
 
**Clear L** (before a vowel): Same as Spanish. The tip of your tongue touches the roof of your mouth.
 
**Dark L** (after a vowel or at the end of a word): Different. The back of your tongue rises. The tip does not need to touch the roof. The sound is deeper and heavier.
 
**Key difference:** Words like *ball*, *milk*, and *feel* end with a dark L. If you use the Spanish L here, native speakers may not understand you.

## 3. The I sound — /ɪ/ /iː/ /aɪ/
 
Spanish has one 'i' sound. English has three.
 
**Short /ɪ/:** Short and relaxed. The mouth is almost closed but not tense. This sound does not exist in Spanish.
 
**Long /iː/:** Like the Spanish 'i', but held longer. The tongue is tense.
 
**Diphthong /aɪ/:** This is NOT a single vowel. It starts with an open 'a' and moves to 'i'. The letter 'i' in words like *time* is always this diphthong.
 
**Key difference:** Spanish speakers often use /iː/ where English needs /ɪ/. This creates confusion — *sit* and *seat* are different words.

## 4. The SH sound — /ʃ/
 
**How to make it:** Push your lips forward a little. Put your tongue near the roof of your mouth without touching it. Push air continuously — like a long, soft 'shhh'.
 
**Key difference:** This sound does not exist in standard Spanish. The important surprise is that **it is written three different ways** in English.
 
**Spelling pattern 1 — sh:** The most common. What you see is what you get.
 
**Spelling pattern 2 — ti / ci / si:** Hidden. The letters *ti*, *ci*, and *si* before an unstressed vowel make the /ʃ/ sound. This is the most common mistake for Spanish speakers.
 
**Spelling pattern 3 — ch (French origin):** A false friend. In Spanish, *ch* = /tʃ/ (like *chico*). In words borrowed from French, *ch* = /ʃ/.

## 5. The J sound — /dʒ/
 
**How to make it:** Put your tongue on the roof of your mouth, like you are going to say 'd'. Then let air out softly, like a 'zh' sound. The two sounds happen very fast together.
 
**Key difference:** The Spanish 'j' is guttural — it comes from the throat (like in *jamón*). The English /dʒ/ is soft and comes from the tongue touching the roof of the mouth. Like the SH sound, it is **written three different ways**.
 
**Spelling pattern 1 — j:** Direct and easy.
 
**Spelling pattern 2 — g before e or i:** A false friend. In Spanish, *g* before e/i makes the guttural /x/ sound (like *gente*). In English, *g* before e/i always makes /dʒ/ (like *gentle*).
 
**Spelling pattern 3 — dge / ge at the end:** The letters *dge* and *ge* at the end of a word make /dʒ/. The 'd' and the final 'e' are both silent.

## Quick reference
 
| Sound | Key rule | Example |
|-------|----------|---------|
| /r/ | No vibration — soft and smooth | *run*, *carry*, *water* |
| /l/ clear | Tongue tip touches roof | *love*, *hello* |
| /l/ dark | Back of tongue rises — deeper sound | *ball*, *milk*, *feel* |
| /ɪ/ short | Short and relaxed — not tense | *sit*, *fish*, *big* |
| /iː/ long | Like Spanish 'i' but longer | *see*, *feet*, *beach* |
| /aɪ/ diphthong | Starts at 'a', slides to 'i' | *time*, *night*, *sky* |
| /ʃ/ | Lips forward, soft continuous air | *she*, *nation*, *machine* |
| /dʒ/ | Tongue to roof, then soft air out | *jump*, *gentle*, *bridge* |
